Namesake and potential for life

William Herschel tried unsuccessfully to name his discovery Georgium Sidus after King George III. Instead, the planet was named for Uranus, the Greek god of the sky, as suggested by Johann Bode.

Uranus' environment is not conducive to life as we know it. The temperatures, pressures, and materials that characterize this planet are most likely too extreme and volatile for organisms to adapt to.

Atmosphere and Exploration

Uranus is an "ice giant." Most of its mass is a hot, dense fluid of "icy" materials—water, methane, and ammonia—above a small rocky core. It has the coldest atmosphere in the solar system.

Voyager 2 is the only spacecraft to have visited Uranus. It flew by in 1986, discovering 10 new moons and two new rings in just a few hours of data collection.
